02 / Shared chemistry

Similar, but not identical.

The chemical similarity of the series is linked to the 4f sublevel being shielded by the 5s and 5p sublevels. The +3 oxidation state is the most common.

◉

The 4f sublevel

The 4f electrons lie within other atomic levels and are shielded by 5s and 5p electrons. As a result, changing 4f occupancy has relatively little effect on shared chemical behaviour.

＋

Oxidation states

+3 is the characteristic state. +2 states occur for Sm, Eu and Yb, while cerium can also form +4, which is useful in selective separation.

◇

Large cations

The relatively large size of Ln ions means coordination numbers above six are common. Bonding in many lanthanide compounds is primarily ionic.

Why separation is difficult: lanthanides commonly occur as +3 ions and have only small differences in their properties in that state. Separation methods use differences in basicity, ionic radius, complex formation or oxidation state.

04 / Separation

Small differences, useful methods.

Several separation routes can be combined because a single property difference may give only partial separation.

Precipitation

Different solubilities and basicities allow one lanthanide compound to precipitate earlier than another.

Thermal decomposition

Heating selected salts can form oxides or basic salts with different solubilities.

Complex formation

Ligands such as EDTA or citrate form complexes that can help separate lanthanides.

Solvent extraction / ion exchange

TBP solvent extraction and ion-exchange resins can separate lanthanides; citrate can be used for elution.

Cerium: a useful exception

Cerium can be separated by selectively oxidising Ce³⁺ to Ce⁴⁺ and then using selective precipitation, crystallisation or extraction.

Ce³⁺ → Ce⁴⁺TBP extractionpH 3–4 example

Ion exchange

Citrate solution passes through a resin column. Smaller lanthanide ions such as Lu³⁺ can form stronger citrate complexes than larger La³⁺, supporting sequential separation.

La³⁺Lu³⁺Citrate / EDTA
05 / Spectral properties

Sharp lines, distinctive colours.

Lanthanide absorption features arise from f–f transitions, with the 4f electrons shielded from surrounding ligand fields.

Absorption & emission

Lanthanides have sharp spectral lines compared with broader transition-metal absorption bands. Selected ions can show fluorescence or luminescence when excited by ultraviolet light.

4f → 4fSharp spectral linesFluorescence / luminescence

Ion colours

Ion colours are related to the electronic configuration, including the relationship between fⁿ and f¹⁴⁻ⁿ configurations.

06 / Applications

From materials to devices.

Lanthanides are used in electronics, lighting, hybrid vehicles, catalysts and medical equipment, as well as in several element-specific technologies.

⌁

Electronics & optics

Hard drives, mobile phones, digital cameras, optical fibres and lasers use lanthanide-containing materials.

✦

Lighting & screens

Lanthanides are used in fluorescent lamps, LCD screens and phosphors, including europium-based red emitters.

↗

Vehicles & catalysts

Applications include hybrid electric motors, hybrid batteries, catalytic converters and cerium compounds in petroleum refining.

⌬

Medical equipment

Applications include X-ray equipment and computed tomography, with lanthanide materials used in selected components and imaging agents.

⚙

Alloys & metals

Mischmetal, alloy additions, jet-engine materials, electrical wires and conductors are among the applications.

◌

Element-specific examples

Cerium is used in lighter flints, erbium in optical fibres and filters, and europium and dysprosium in neutron-absorbing applications.
